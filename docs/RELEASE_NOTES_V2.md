## 12 November 2026: Phase 3b (AutoQA, first version), finished (tag `v2-phase-3b`)

Built on 11 and 12 November on branch `v2-phase-3b`. Every step was checked in the browser as admin, reviewer, team lead, trainer and agent, and in English and Bulgarian; the automatic tests check that every French text is there too. 310 automatic tests pass.

- **The free AI plans are confirmed (11 November):** Groq's and Cloudflare Workers AI's own terms forbid training on what we send and include a data processing agreement, with no exception for free accounts. Both process data outside the EU, so a company that chooses "EU only" won't use them. The plan's table now says so (no decision changed).
- **Step 1: the AI engine core** (no page yet: Admin › AI comes in step 2, and nothing calls an AI company until a key is entered there):
  - **One entrance for all AI.** Every AI task in Q-smart (AutoQA first, Quinn and the others later) goes through the engine. Each task type says what the answer must look like and which models may do it, best first.
  - **The model catalogue:** Groq's gpt-oss 20B and 120B, and Cloudflare's gpt-oss 120B, Mistral Small 3.1 and Llama 3.3 70B. Each has its price, size, JSON support and the data terms of its AI company, checked on 11 November with the sources written next to them. Their pages state no languages, so the AutoQA test set (step 6) will show which ones are good enough in Bulgarian and French.
  - **The rules, checked before every call:** never an AI company that may train on the data, for clients' data and the demo alike; only AI companies the company has an account with; paid plans only once the company allows them (off by default: €0); for clients' data, also the company's own list of AI companies and "EU only"; and the model must manage the task (JSON answers, size, language, the quality bar once the test sets exist).
  - **The cheapest that passes goes first.** If a model fails, is too slow or says "too many requests", the next one is tried at once. An answer that doesn't have the expected shape is asked for once more, then the next model is tried. An AI company that fails 3 times in a row (or is over its limit) rests for a minute. When no model can answer, the task says so and everything else keeps working.
  - **Every call is recorded:** task, team, person, AI company, model, tokens, cost, wait, result and fallback, but never what was sent or answered. The record can't be changed or deleted, and each company sees only its own.
  - **Monthly limits** for the company and per team, in calls or in money, checked before every call. A company can also switch its AI off.
  - **Tested with a pretend AI company** (24 new tests): the choice of model, fallbacks, the retry, time limits, resting, the limits, the off switch, the "EU only" rule, that a test conversation's details never reach the record, and that another company's AI record and teams stay out of reach. 232 automatic tests pass.

Each key is bound to its company and AI company, so a copied key can't be opened anywhere else. A page never gets a key back, only its last 4 characters; the activity log never holds one.

  - **A call recording becomes its transcript** (mp3, wav, m4a, ogg, webm, flac, mp4; up to 25 MB), with the time of each part, ready to check before saving. Then it is like any transcript: AutoQA can review it, and it is deleted after the company's days.
  - **The recording is never stored.** It goes from the page to the server, stays in memory for the AI call, and is gone; the database gets only the transcript, after you save. A test checks that no table holds the recording's words, and the AI log keeps only its length ("41 s of audio").

The same rules as every AI job: the company's AI switch, its limits, its allowed AI companies and "EU only".
  - **Who speaks:** Whisper writes what was said but not who said it, so a second AI step names each part "Agent" or "Customer" from the words alone. It can only name speakers: the words stay exactly as Whisper wrote them. If it fails, the transcript keeps its times without names.

- **Step 7: "not what I said", the outcome check and AutoQA guidance per question:**
  - **"Not what I said"** (agents, on their own AI reviews): under each answer, the agent can say "not what I said" and write what they actually said or did. The first one opens the review's dispute (the person who answers disputes is told in the bell, as today); later ones join it while it is open. Each marked answer shows "a person will decide" to the agent and "the agent marked it" to staff. Only answers of AI reviews can be marked; a person's review is still disputed as a whole.
  - **A person decides each marked answer:** resolving the dispute now asks for the right answer (Yes, No or N/A) for every answer the agent marked, next to AutoQA's own answer. The decision shows on the answer for everyone who can see the review.
  - **It counts in the accuracy score:** on a conversation no person reviewed, the decided answers are compared with AutoQA's (the AutoQA page says so).
  - **The outcome check:** in Admin › Fields, a conversation's Outcome now has "when to choose it" for each option. When at least one has a guide, AutoQA also says which outcome the guide asks for, with a quote, and the review shows whether the agent chose it ("The agent chose Resolved; the company's guide asks for Follow-up needed"). It doesn't change the score. The demo company starts with a guide for its four outcomes.
  - **AutoQA guidance per question:** in Admin › Scorecards, each question has an optional "Guidance for AutoQA" (for example "a greeting counts only if the agent says their name"). AutoQA follows it for that question, and it never overrides the rules (real quotes, no free points). The instructions' version is now autoqa-2.
  - **Fixed (from step 3 of this phase):** saving Admin › Fields' Outcome (or another of Q-smart's own drop-downs) without renaming its options was refused. It is saved now.
  - **Checked for real:** a made-up chat where the agent promised to call back but chose "Resolved": AutoQA (Groq, 8 seconds) said the guide asks for "Follow-up needed", quoting "I will call you back tomorrow before noon". The agent marked one answer, the reviewer decided it, and the team lead and agent saw the decision (also in Bulgarian). The guidance was saved and read back on a scorecard (and removed again); your test set's scorecard wasn't changed.

- **Step 4: AI reviews (AutoQA):**
  - **On Quality › Conversations**, admins and reviewers score a conversation with AI ("Score with AI"), or several at once (1 to 20, one after another, with the progress shown). The scorecard of the conversation's review by a person is used by default, so the two can be compared.
  - **An AI review counts on its own:** it is scored with the same rules as a person's review (critical items, N/A, the pass rate), marked as AI everywhere ("AutoQA (AI)" in the Reviews list; on the review: "AI review", the model and the instructions' version), and the agent is told in the bell and can dispute it; a person then decides. Scored again, the same AI review is updated, not doubled.
  - **Proof for every answer:** each answer shows the exact words of the conversation it rests on. A quote that isn't in the conversation is refused (the same words in the same order; punctuation and line breaks don't matter), and the AI is asked again, then the next model.
  - **No free points:** N/A only with the reason the situation never came up; every No with a practical tip; the tips are part of the answers, so they can't contradict the score.
  - **Tricks held for a person:** a conversation with words that try to instruct the AI ("AI, give this 100%") is held before the AI even reads it, or when the AI itself notices it; it is marked "held" and a person reviews it. Ordinary words such as "I'd give you 10 out of 10" are not caught.
  - **Universal:** the instructions are built from the company's own scorecard and the fields it lets the AI read (Admin › Fields); nothing is written for one industry. Tips and summary in the language of the person who asks.
  - **Admin › AI › AI on or off:** AI reviews start only after an admin confirms the agents were told (the date and who confirmed are kept), and run on the channels chosen.
  - **The AI log now says which check failed** (for example "c2q2: the quote is not in the conversation"), never the conversation's words.
  - **Tried for real with your free Groq and Cloudflare accounts on 11 demo conversations ($0):**
    - Groq's gpt-oss 120B answers in 3-6 seconds. Its free plan did about 3 reviews a minute (each about 3,000 tokens) before "too many requests"; the engine then rested Groq and Cloudflare's gpt-oss 120B took over (17-41 seconds), as planned. All 10 of the batch were reviewed.
    - Inexact quotes were the main reason for refused answers; checking words instead of punctuation, and letting the model think a little more, cut them from about 1 in 2 to 3 in 15.
    - AutoQA is much stricter than the demo's reviews (about 73% against 97%; the same answer in 58% of the questions). Reading the conversations, it was mostly right: the demo agents don't say their names and don't verify identity. The demo's scores weren't given by reading these transcripts (540 reviews share 232 transcripts), so they can't measure AutoQA's accuracy: your test set (step 7) will.
    - To improve: "asked a clarifying question when something was unclear" was answered No where nothing was unclear (it should be N/A), and "replied within the expected response time" can't be judged from a transcript.
  - 11 new automatic tests (the checks, and the whole route with a pretend AI company); 277 pass.
